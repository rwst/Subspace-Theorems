# Evertse 1984: sums of S-units and linear recurrences

J.-H. Evertse, *On sums of `S`-units and linear recurrences*, Compositio Mathematica **53**
(1984), 225–244.

This directory is for formalizing the whole paper on top of the libraries of this repository. It
is its own `lean_lib` (`Evertse1984`), held to the same rules (no `sorry`, no `set_option`, std3
axioms, fine-grained imports, module system, Mathlib root namespaces), and is its own Palomar
submission, like `CorvajaZannier2004/` and `AdamczewskiBugeaud2007/`.

## Certification

`lake test -- comparator/evertse-1984.json` certifies the 14 theorems of
`comparator/evertse-1984.txt` (every numbered result of the paper, including the cited Theorem 4,
which is `DiophantineApproximation` Layer 6.4; Theorem 1, Theorem 3 and Corollaries 3, 4 in two
forms each) against the flat, Mathlib-only `ChallengeEvertse1984.lean` (316 lines, 16 KiB),
generated by `scripts/make-challenge.py --paper Evertse1984`, with proofs re-exported by
`SolutionEvertse1984.lean`. Comparator passes within the three standard axioms, and a negative
control (a weakened hypothesis of Corollary 4 in the challenge) is rejected. The metadata is
`formalization.yaml` in this directory.

The paper sits between this repository and Laurent, *Équations diophantiennes exponentielles*
(Invent. Math. 78, 1984): Laurent's Lemma 7 is Theorem 2 below, word for word, and it is his only
quantitative use of the Subspace Theorem.

## Conventions

- `‖α‖_v` is `w α ^ mult w` at an infinite place `w` and `FinitePlace.mk P α` at a prime `P`:
  Mathlib's normalization, the one of the product formula, which is Evertse's.
- `S` is a `Finset` of primes; the infinite places are always included, as in Evertse.
- Evertse's `T ⊆ S` is any `Set (AbsoluteValue K ℝ)`, of which only the places of `S∞ ∪ S` count
  (`placeProd_inter_relevantPlaces`). `T = ∅` is allowed; the inequality is then the product
  formula.
- `‖x‖`, the largest absolute value of a conjugate of a coordinate, is `⨆ i, house (x i)`.
- Tuples are `x : ι → K` with `IsIntegral ℤ (x i)`, `ι` any `Fintype` (Evertse's `n + 1`
  coordinates).

## Notes on the proof of Theorem 2

- Evertse restricts to points with a fixed ordering of the `‖x_k‖_v`; only the index of the
  largest one at each place of `T` is used, so the cases here are the functions
  `r : AbsoluteValue K ℝ → Option ι` defined exactly on `T` (finitely many).
- In the step, the second application of the induction hypothesis is over the places of `T` whose
  largest coordinate lies outside the shorter sum. Evertse treats the case where there are none
  separately; the induction hypothesis with that empty set covers it.

## Notes on the proof of Theorem 1

- Evertse normalizes with a `λ ∈ K` that need not be an `S`-unit (Schmidt's argument, through the
  class group) and compares the admissibility products through the ideal of the coordinates. An
  `S`-unit leaves both the admissibility product and `H_S(x) = ∏_{v ∈ S∞ ∪ S} max_k ‖x_k‖_v`
  unchanged (the `S`-product formula), and exists by the covering radius of the `S`-unit lattice of
  `ArithmeticHeights` 6.5; the bound comes out in terms of `H_S(x) ≥ H(X)`, which is enough.
- `ε = [K : ℚ] (1 - d) / 2`, as in the paper.
- The hypothesis `c > 0` is not used.

## Notes on the proof of Lemma 2

- Evertse's ideal norms `N((f(r), g(s)))` are replaced by places: `∏ (x_i - y_j)` lies in the
  ideal of the `S`-integers generated by `∏ x_i` and `∏ y_j` (`exists_prod_product_sub_eq`), so
  `Q / F` is an `S`-integer and `∏_{S∞ ∪ S} ‖F‖ ≤ ∏_{S∞ ∪ S} ‖Q‖`.
- The zeros of `f`, `g` need only be `S`-integers, not `S`-units; `S` is enlarged inside the proof
  so that the caller need not choose it.
- The hypothesis `0 ≤ γ < 1 / (m + n + 2)` is used exactly as in the paper, through
  `d = (m + n + 1) γ / (1 - γ) < 1`.

## Notes on the proof of Theorem 3

- Stated as: for every finite `S`, finitely many `r > s` with `u_s ≠ 0` and `u_r / u_s` zero or
  an `S`-unit (equivalent to `P_K(u_r / u_s) → ∞`). `S` is enlarged inside the proof so that the
  `α_i` are `S`-units and the coefficients of the `f_i` are `S`-integers.
- Step A uses Theorem 1 with `d = 1/2` and heights only (no choice of place).
- Case `σ ≠ 1` replaces Evertse's single place by the logarithm of the product over the places
  where `|α_i / α_{σ i}| > 1`; the relations telescope to `r · log H(α_i / α_{σ i}) ≤ 2 log(C r^N)`.
- The passage to a splitting field reuses `DiophantineApproximation`'s `map_mem_unit_iff` (a unit
  of `K` for `S` is a unit of `L` for the primes above `S`). A recurrence is given by its
  representation `u_k = ∑_i f_i(k) α_i ^ k` in `L`; deriving that representation from a linear
  recurrence relation is not formalized here.
- Nothing of the paper is open.
