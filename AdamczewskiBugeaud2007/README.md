# Adamczewski–Bugeaud 2007: the complexity of algebraic numbers in integer bases

B. Adamczewski and Y. Bugeaud, *On the complexity of algebraic numbers I. Expansions in integer
bases*, Annals of Mathematics **165** (2007), 547–565.

This directory is for formalizing the whole paper on top of the two libraries of this repository.
It is its own `lean_lib` (`AdamczewskiBugeaud2007`), held to the same rules (no `sorry`, no
`set_option`, std3 axioms, fine-grained imports, module system, Mathlib root namespaces), and it
carries its own Palomar submission: its own flat Challenge, comparator config and
`formalization.yaml` (§ "Certification" below).

It started as the port of `~/math/lean-code/AB/` (Ralf Stephan with Claude Code, CC0, relicensed
here under the repository's Apache 2.0). That directory recorded the paper mostly as cited
`axiom`s, which this repository does not admit. Its content was handled three ways:

- **Ported**: definitions with no counterpart in the repository, rebased on Mathlib's namespaces
  and API, with their proofs.
- **Already a theorem here**: Theorem 1, Theorem 5 and their vocabulary are proved in
  `DiophantineApproximation` (Layers 7.4–7.5). They are consumed by name, not duplicated.
- **Cited axioms, now theorems**: every axiom of `AB/` is proved here or in DA. The one exception
  is Theorem E, which DA proves in its own normalization; see below.

The rest of the paper is planned below (§ "Plan: the rest of the paper").

## Fidelity notes

- **Cobham's bound needs no automata.** DA's roadmap lists automatic sequences as out of scope for
  want of finite automata and Cobham's theorem. The bound `p(n) = O(n)` follows from the finite
  kernel directly. The block of level `m` at `q` is determined by the vector `(s q)_{s ∈ K}`. A
  factor of length `L ≤ k ^ m` is determined by its offset and two consecutive blocks. So
  `p(L) ≤ k |K → range a| ^ 2 L`.
- **The paper's Condition `(∗)_w` includes non-periodicity**; `Function.IsStammering` does not.
  The criteria take `¬ IsEventuallyPeriodic a` as a separate hypothesis, and the automatic ⇒
  stammering statement needs none.
- **`AB/` encoded `(∗)_w` with `⌊w s⌋`**, a weakening; DA's word-based definition has the paper's
  length `⌈w s⌉`.
- **Hensel expansions start at `k = 0`** in `HenselExpansion.lean`. The paper's
  `∑_{k ≥ -m} a k p ^ k`, with Condition `(∗)_w` on `(a k)_{k ≥ 1}`, is a rational multiple of
  `Padic.ofDigits` of those digits plus a rational, so the statements are equivalent;
  `HenselDigits.lean` states Theorems 1B and 6 for the expansion of a given `α ∈ ℚ_p` as printed. `AB.PadicIrrational x` is inlined as
  `x ∉ Set.range ((↑) : ℚ → ℚ_[p])`.
- **Theorem 6 uses DA's Subspace Theorem with algebraic coefficients** (Layer 6.3). The coefficient
  field is `ℚ⟮α⟯ ⊆ ℚ_p` with the norm of `ℚ_p`, so no Schlickewei-specific statement is needed.
  The endgame is simpler than over `ℝ`: `p ^ s θ → 0` in `ℚ_p` for every `θ`.
- **Theorem E is not restated.** The paper quotes it (Evertse's form) and applies it; the
  formalization applies DA 6.2/6.3 instead. A paper-faithful restatement would have to bridge three
  things: raw `AbsoluteValue` places versus typed ones, the missing `mult` weights, and Evertse's
  `L²` norm at `∞`. Each change costs a constant, which the ε-slack and Northcott absorb. That is
  about 200–400 lines with no mathematical payoff. (`AB.subspace_theorem_E` is sound, since it
  carries `hS_place`. Over fields with a complex place it is weaker than DA's theorem.)
- **Dropped**: the `ℕ`-valued duplicates `AS.kKernel`/`AS.IsAutomatic`, and
  `AS.isAutomatic'_iff_faithful_encoding`, which only served lean-code's `ℕ`-encoded consumers.

### Findings from the reading

- ⚠ **Theorem 7 is false as printed.** It speaks of "a sequence of integers `u`". Take `p = 5`,
  `b = 2`, `z k = [k is a power of 5]` and `u k = z (k + 1) + 2 (1 − z k)` for `k ≥ 1`, with
  values in `{0, 1, 2, 3}`. Then `∑ u k 2^{-k} = (2Z − 1) + 2(1 − Z) = 1`, where
  `Z = ∑ z k 2^{-k}`. In characteristic 5, `f = ∑ z k X^k` satisfies `f^5 = f − X`, and
  `∑ u k X^k = X⁻¹ (f − X) + 2 (X / (1 − X) − f)`, which is algebraic. It is not rational, since
  `u` is not eventually periodic. So both objects are algebraic but not both rational. The
  proof's step "real rational ⇒ `u` eventually periodic" needs `u k` to be base-`b` digits. The
  step "`∑ u k X^k` algebraic ⇒ `u` is `p`-automatic" needs `u k < p`. The statement that holds is
  the one for `u k < min b p`, and the counterexample is formalizable without Christol, since
  `f^5 = f − X` is direct.
- **Theorem 4's proof has a small gap.** The letter of maximal growth `a` is chosen over the whole
  alphabet, but recurrence says nothing about letters that never occur. The fix is to take the
  maximum over the letters that occur. This is harmless, because `φ` maps occurring letters to
  words of occurring letters.
- **Theorem 5 does not need non-periodicity.** If `a` is eventually periodic, `α ∈ ℚ(β)`
  already. So the formal statement can drop that part of `(∗)_w`, which also makes Theorems
  1A–4A shorter.
- **The paper's proof of Theorem 2 via Cobham's uniform-morphism representation is not needed.**
  The kernel bound in `AutomaticComplexity.lean` replaces it.

  **G-7 done (2026-09-28).**
  - **The published version is uncorrected.** The Annals text (vol. 165, p. 563) has the arXiv
    wording verbatim, "the sequence of integers `u = (u_k)_{k ≥ 1}`", with no bound on `u_k`. No
    erratum was found (web search; none on the journal's or Adamczewski's pages).
  - **The authors corrected it silently, in Adamczewski's habilitation.** B. Adamczewski, *Une
    approche arithmétique des systèmes de numération*, HDR, Lyon 1, defended 8 April 2010
    (`~/math/lean-code/papers/AdamczewskiDiss.pdf`, §7.2). Théorème 7.1, credited to
    [AdBu07c] = this paper: for an aperiodic `(a_n)` with values in `{0, 1, …, p − 1}`, if
    `∑ a_n t^n ∈ 𝔽_p((t))` is algebraic over `𝔽_p(t)`, then `∑ a_n / p^n` is transcendental, and
    conversely. That is the base `b = p`, with the digit bound, stated as a dichotomy for
    aperiodic sequences. The two-base form `u_k < min(b, p)` recorded above is ours, and it is
    what the paper's proof gives.
  - **No other source restates it.** None of the ~285 papers in `~/math/lean-code/papers/`
    (text-searched; 14 scanned files have no text, none relevant) restates Theorem 7. That
    includes Bugeaud's 2012 book, chapters 2–10, which never mentions Christol, and
    Adamczewski–Bugeaud 2010/2011.
  - **Verdict: the error is known to the authors**, but the counterexample appears nowhere. It is
    worth a remark, not a paper.
  - **Christol's theorem is formalized nowhere we could find.** Mathlib has nothing on it or on
    Cartier operators. The lean-code corpus only mentions it
    (`CITED/AdamczewskiFaverjonRegular.lean`), and a web search finds no Lean, Isabelle or Coq
    development. So WP12 builds it here, and only the direction algebraic ⇒ `p`-automatic is
    needed.
  - **What to formalize:** Theorem 7 in the corrected form `u_k < min(b, p)`, which also gives the
    HDR's Théorème 7.1 at `b = p`, plus WP11, the counterexample to the printed form.
- **WP12, Christol's theorem, algebraic ⇒ automatic** (`CartierOperator.lean`, `Christol.lean`;
  CKMR80 Théorème 1, (iii) ⇒ (i), proof in §7). For every finite field `K` with `q` elements:
  `PowerSeries.finite_kKernel_coeff_of_isAlgebraic`, the `q`-kernel of the coefficients of an
  `f ∈ K⟦X⟧` algebraic over `K[X]` is finite.
  - The Frobenius identity comes from Mathlib's `FiniteField.PowerSeries.expand_card`
    (`f ^ q = f (X ^ q)`), so `Λ_r (A ^ q B) = A Λ_r B` is a coefficient computation.
  - The relation among the `f ^ (q ^ i)`: an integral multiple `y f` generates a finitely
    generated `K[X]`-module, so `s + 1` Frobenius powers are dependent (strong rank condition).
  - **No Laurent series.** CKMR80 put `g = f / a₀` in `K((X))`. Multiplying through instead, the
    finite `Λ`-stable set is `{s : a₀ s = ∑_(i ≤ n) c_i f ^ (q ^ i), deg c_i ≤ N}`. It is finite
    because `a₀ ≠ 0` in the domain `K⟦X⟧`, and a Cartier step is: multiply by `a₀ ^ (q − 1)`,
    eliminate `c₀ a₀ f` with the relation, apply `Λ_r`. Degrees stay polynomial inside `K⟦X⟧`
    (`PowerSeries.IsBoundedBy`).
  - About 430 lines against the estimate of 1500–2500: the plan budgeted for Laurent series and
    `RatFunc`, which the multiplied-through set avoids.
